EXP-0002 round 4 — connector-ink warp contact sheet

W1 (naturalness) is a human verdict and is not graded here. The claim to judge is “your ink, moved”. Read each panel like this: the grey line is the ink as it was drawn, in the place it was drawn. The pale blue halo is that same ink carried rigidly to where the boxes are now, with no blending whatsoever — that is the ideal. The red line is what the model ships. Wherever red sits inside the blue halo, the ink is untouched; wherever they separate, the blend has done work, and the sand-coloured band marks the only stretch it is permitted to do that work in. The question for you is whether the red line still reads as the same hand as the grey one.

Start with arc/identity and wiggle/identity: nothing has moved, so there is a single line and no band at all. That is invariant I6 — with nothing moved the output is bitwise the ink. Everything you see in the other panels is the price of motion the creator asked for, not the price of recognition.

Final model: similarity warp, plus a Hermite blend at each end whose arc is exactly what the turn at that end demands — so with nothing moved there is no blend at all. An edge anchor's facing is the departure the creator drew, stored in the edge's local frame and carried rigidly with it (D26/D27). Departure stub ratio 1.50, turn room 5.0, blend cap 0.35 per end, minimum ink radius 12.0 world units. Orange ticks mark where each blend region ends; the orange dotted spur at each anchor is the departure stub. Full algorithm in CANONICAL-ALGORITHM.md, all sweeps in report.txt.

Per D26 an edge anchor's facing is fixed and may oppose the chord; those cases are marked REVERSED and their U-turn is expected behaviour, not a defect. The creator's remedy — switching that end to a centre bind — is rendered in the W7 section at the bottom.
